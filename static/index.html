What this prototype is

ONE CALL.

The same emergencies, played twice in one city.

This is a simulated, Hyderabad-flavoured city with 8 hospitals and a shared fleet of ambulances. Every patient happens twice, on the same roads at the same moment: once handled today's way (drive to the nearest hospital), once handled by RightCall. Nothing here is real data. What is real is the decision engine, which only sees what a real fleet could see.

The problem

Hospitals rarely publish when their emergency department is full. Crews drive to the nearest one, wait at the gate, and sometimes leave again with the patient still aboard. Phoning ahead helps, but crews phone the nearest hospital first, and that is usually the busy one.

01

The fleet already knows

Every ambulance reports GPS. From that alone we can count who is parked at a hospital gate, time each handover, and spot a crew that leaves with the patient still on board.

02

Rank on that evidence

Score = drive time + estimated wait + 40 × turn-away risk. Recent observations count more; old ones fade. No evidence means "unknown", and the call decides.

03

Confirm with one call

The crew phones the top-ranked hospital, not the nearest. If it says no, the next one gets the call. Sickest patients are served first, and a big saving en route triggers a re-route.
